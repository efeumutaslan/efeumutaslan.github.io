I'm writing this on the evening of August 11th, around 10 PM. I had reached the final step of my blog page - the most exciting part, the publishing phase. However, thanks to my great luck, I was unaware that a 16-hour internet outage was imminent. Since my desktop computer doesn't have a Wi-Fi card, I couldn't use my phone as a hotspot. Then I remembered my girlfriend's laptop. The only problem was that the actual files were on my Windows computer and my girlfriend uses a MacBook Air. Since we just bought the laptop, we hadn't gotten a dock yet, so a simple USB drive wouldn't work. I tried connecting the computers via Bluetooth, but this solution was quite inadequate and unfortunately, I couldn't succeed.

Then I remembered the USB port on my old modem. However, when I checked my new modem, I unfortunately realized it didn't have a USB port. So I couldn't share files through the modem. As options dwindled, the hours were rapidly passing. As a last resort, I thought I could solve this problem by enabling file sharing on the local network on both computers. But before I explain this thought, I should mention that I had already considered the "Why don't you use your phone as a USB drive" idea that might come to your mind, but the cables I had weren't compatible with my desktop computer.

Now let's get back to the file sharing solution. While implementing my idea of creating a shared folder on both computers, I encountered several problems. And unfortunately, I couldn't establish file sharing between these computers on the same network either. But I persisted and wanted to share this post and publish my site tonight. My story was like Professor Leonard Kleinrock's attempt years ago to connect from one computer to another - when trying to write "LOGIN," the SRI computer crashed when he got to the letter G. There was no internet; they were trying to create the internet. An untimely maintenance had brought the same situation upon me.

So how did I publish this post to you? First, I could have admitted defeat, downloaded all the files to the laptop again, and transferred the ready code. But that wouldn't have been fun or satisfying at all. I remembered a developer who managed to fit the Snake game into a QR code years ago. If a game can fit into a QR code, I thought I could fit a few files too. I converted all the file contents to a text file and turned it into a QR code. By scanning the QR code on the laptop, I copied and pasted the project.

The only thing I was missing was the image files other than SVG. I had them because I had prepared them earlier with a cloud-based program. Maybe rewriting the entire project would have been 5 times faster than what I did. But then Professor Leonard Kleinrock could have gotten faster results by flying the documents. But then you wouldn't be reading this post right now. Lo and Behold!
